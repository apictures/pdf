PDF Tips & Guides

Short, practical guides to help you prepare documents, choose the right tool, and check the results. Start with the FreePDF tools.

FreePDF guides · Updated October 2026

1. How to merge multiple PDF files

Merging is useful when related pages are spread across separate documents—for example, a report and its appendices. Check the page order before creating the final file.

  1. Open the Merge PDF tool.
  2. Select the PDF files you want to combine.
  3. Arrange or select them in the intended order if the tool offers that control.
  4. Create the merged file, then open it and check the first, middle, and last pages.

Tip: use clear filenames such as report-part-1.pdf and report-part-2.pdf to make the intended order easier to track.

2. How to extract selected pages from a PDF

Splitting can help you share only the pages someone needs or separate a chapter from a large document.

  1. Choose Split PDF.
  2. Select your PDF and choose the pages you need.
  3. Run the extraction and open the resulting file.
  4. Confirm that the selected pages and their order are correct before sharing.

For long documents, check the page numbers in the PDF viewer first; the printed page number on a page may differ from the viewer's page index.

3. When should you use image OCR?

OCR (optical character recognition) attempts to detect text in an image or scanned page. It can make text easier to copy, search, or edit, but it is not always exact.

Improve OCR results

  • Use a sharp, well-lit image with the page aligned as straight as possible.
  • Crop out unrelated background and avoid glare or shadows.
  • Use a sufficiently large image and keep text contrast high.
  • Proofread names, dates, numbers, and punctuation after recognition.

Handwriting, unusual fonts, low-resolution scans, and mixed-language pages may produce errors. Do not rely on OCR output without checking it.

4. Tips for converting PDFs to editable documents

PDFs are designed to preserve page appearance, while Word and spreadsheet formats are designed for editing. A conversion may therefore change line breaks, tables, fonts, spacing, or page layout.

  • Start with a text-based PDF when possible; scanned pages may require OCR.
  • For tables, check every row and column after conversion.
  • Compare page count and important formatting with the original.
  • Keep the original file until you have reviewed the converted copy.

6. How to convert JPG images to PDF

Converting photos or image pages to PDF can make them easier to share as one document. FreePDF's JPG to PDF tool supports selecting multiple images so you can combine them into a PDF.

  1. Open the JPG to PDF guide or go directly to the JPG to PDF tool.
  2. Select the JPG, JPEG, or PNG images you want to include.
  3. Check the image order and orientation before converting.
  4. Create the PDF, download it, and open it to check that all pages appear correctly.

Tip: use clear, well-lit images and keep the original photos until you've checked the PDF output.

5. Protect your documents and privacy

Choose tools and services carefully when working with sensitive files. FreePDF is designed to process supported files in your browser, but the page loads third-party software libraries. Your browser, extensions, and those third-party services operate under their own terms.

  • Do not upload confidential documents to public issue trackers or support forums.
  • Keep a backup of important originals.
  • Use a strong, unique password when a document-protection feature asks you to create one.
  • Test protected files to ensure the password and permissions behave as expected.
